Develop a correlative workflow that uses multicolor single-molecule localization microscopy (SMLM) to identify and quantify glycocalyx perturbations and human coronavirus RNA replication structures in live cells, then rapidly vitrifies and targets the same sites for cryo-FIB lamella preparation and cryo-electron tomography (cryo-ET) to reveal their 3D ultrastructure.

How do pharmacological glycosylation inhibitors alter the nanoscale organization of the cell-surface glycocalyx, and which specific ultrastructural changes (membrane curvature, coat assemblies, vesicle/cisternal architecture) emerge at those sites in cryo-ET?

Where (relative to glycocalyx state and membrane trafficking landmarks) do coronavirus RNA puncta detected by multicolor SMLM correspond to distinct 3D replication organelle morphologies in cryo-ET, and can inhibitor-driven glycocalyx remodeling shift these morphologies in a predictable way?


Interest level: (1=not interesting, 5=very interesting): 2

Project 45

Nanoscale-Resolved Fate of Exhaled Droplets: Linking Fluorophore Super-Resolution Microscopy to Aerosol Turbulence and Evaporation

Develop a fluorescence labeling and super-resolution imaging workflow to visualize, localize, and quantify nanoscale composition/structure in individual exhaled particles (fresh droplets to aged droplet nuclei) while simultaneously characterizing their size evolution and transport under controlled turbulent airflow and humidity.

How do evaporation rate, humidity, and turbulent shear reshape the nanoscale spatial distribution of mucins/viral-RNA surrogates/salts within single exhaled particles, and do these internal rearrangements create distinct “microenvironments” that alter molecular stability?

Can super-resolution–derived nanoscale morphology and component partitioning be incorporated into aerosol-physics models (settling, dispersion, resuspension) to predict which particle sub-populations most efficiently preserve and transport biological payloads across realistic airflow conditions (e.g., speech, wind instruments, ventilation)?


Interest level: (1=not interesting, 5=very interesting): 1
