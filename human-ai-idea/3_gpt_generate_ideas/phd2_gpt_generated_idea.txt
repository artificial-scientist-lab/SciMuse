Develop and adapt machine-learning morphology pipelines originally built for Euclid-scale galaxy structure inference to quantify 3D tissue remodeling and lesion-environment mechanics in regenerating spiny mouse CNS/skin, enabling predictive, low-scar regeneration biomarkers from imaging.

Research Question & Novel Results (2 points):  
1) **Can galaxy-inspired structural parameterization (e.g., multi-component “bulge/disk”-like decompositions and shape/anisotropy inference) yield a compact, interpretable set of quantitative descriptors for regenerating lesions that predicts functional outcomes and scarring better than conventional histology scores?**  
2) **Can Bayesian forward modeling of imaging data disentangle viewing-angle/sectioning biases and measurement noise to reveal the true spatiotemporal dynamics of ECM mechanics (e.g., proteoglycan-driven stiffness/organization) that permit spiny-mouse CNS regeneration, identifying causal microenvironment features to target in non-regenerative mammals?**
